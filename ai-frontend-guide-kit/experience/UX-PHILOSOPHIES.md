# UX Philosophies

A philosophy is the intent behind every trade-off: what the experience optimizes for and what it is willing to sacrifice. Pick **one primary** (a secondary at most) and explain why; the discarded alternative belongs in the brief. `navigation_model` feeds the map template.

## 1 · Functional clarity
Goal: the user understands and acts with minimal effort. Structure: obvious hierarchy, one primary action per screen. Nav: simple, task-labeled. Density: medium. Rhythm: calm. Interaction: standard affordances, immediate feedback. Emotion: confidence. Pros: low confusion, fast to build. Risks: can feel flat, undifferentiated. Not for: brand-led or emotional experiences.

## 2 · Operational efficiency
Goal: do frequent tasks fast. Structure: dashboard/shell, shortcuts, bulk actions. Nav: persistent, role-based. Density: high, deliberate. Rhythm: steady. Interaction: instant, keyboard-first. Emotion: control. Pros: speed, productivity. Risks: overwhelming for newcomers; needs onboarding. Not for: one-off visitors.

## 3 · Discovery
Goal: let people find things they did not know they wanted. Structure: categories, collections, related items. Nav: faceted, exploratory. Density: medium. Rhythm: browseable. Interaction: filters, previews, search. Emotion: curiosity. Pros: engagement and depth. Risks: choice overload; needs good curation. Not for: single-purpose task pages.

## 4 · Trust and authority
Goal: be believed. Structure: evidence-first (proof, numbers, sources, people). Nav: restricted to reduce doubt. Density: medium. Rhythm: deliberate. Interaction: quiet, no gimmicks. Emotion: security. Pros: converts complex/high-price offers. Risks: dry, slow; proof must be real. Not for: playful consumer products.

## 5 · Narrative and emotion
Goal: make the experience memorable. Structure: pacing, chapters, reveals. Nav: guided scroll or story steps. Density: low/medium. Rhythm: cinematic. Interaction: motion with purpose, progressive media. Emotion: awe, empathy. Pros: differentiation and recall. Risks: performance, reduced-motion, SEO depth, data cost. Not for: dashboards or repeated tasks.

## 6 · Transparency
Goal: show exactly what happens, when and why. Structure: states, logs, history, pricing clarity. Nav: explicit. Density: medium/high. Rhythm: honest. Interaction: confirmations that inform, not decorate. Emotion: control. Pros: trust in tools and AI. Risks: noise; needs information design. Not for: brand moments.

## 7 · Professional control
Goal: expert users feel in command. Structure: dense panels, customization, commands. Nav: hubs + in-page. Density: high. Rhythm: fast. Interaction: keyboard, shortcuts, multi-select, undo. Emotion: mastery. Pros: power users love it. Risks: steep learning curve. Not for: general public.

## 8 · Progressive simplicity
Goal: reveal complexity only when needed. Structure: essentials first, advanced hidden. Nav: shallow first levels. Density: low → medium. Rhythm: gentle. Interaction: progressive disclosure, sensible defaults. Emotion: ease. Pros: approachable power. Risks: hidden features get lost; search/settings needed. Not for: expert-first consoles without shortcuts.

## 9 · Personalization
Goal: the experience adapts to the person or context. Structure: dynamic sections, saved preferences. Nav: personalized shortcuts. Density: variable. Rhythm: adaptive. Interaction: sensible defaults + control over them. Emotion: relevance. Pros: engagement. Risks: filter bubbles, privacy, cold start. Not for: anonymous low-data contexts.

## 10 · Technical depth
Goal: be precise and verifiable for technical users. Structure: specs, diagrams, references, code, versions. Nav: sidebar + in-page + search. Density: high. Rhythm: reference. Interaction: copy, expand, link, compare. Emotion: credibility. Pros: wins technical audiences. Risks: alienates non-technical buyers. Not for: consumer marketing pages.

## 11 · Community
Goal: belonging and contribution. Structure: profiles, feeds, threads, reputation. Nav: hub. Density: medium. Rhythm: alive. Interaction: post, react, mention, moderate. Emotion: connection. Pros: retention and UGC. Risks: empty/social problems; moderation cost. Not for: single-player tools.

## 12 · Conversion without friction
Goal: one clear yes. Structure: single action, no competing CTAs. Nav: removed or minimal. Density: low. Rhythm: persuasive. Interaction: short forms, trust signals near commitment. Emotion: readiness. Pros: measurable. Risks: dark patterns; brand erosion. Not for: exploratory content sites.

## 13 · Visual exploration
Goal: let the work/content speak. Structure: galleries, full-bleed media, minimal chrome. Nav: visual, filter-driven. Density: low. Rhythm: showcase. Interaction: hover/scroll reveals. Emotion: admiration. Pros: portfolio/media impact. Risks: accessibility, performance, information hiding. Not for: data-heavy tools.

## 14 · Editorial
Goal: reading as an experience. Structure: grid, columns, typography rhythm. Nav: categories, internal links. Density: medium. Rhythm: readable. Interaction: quiet, in-page nav, related reads. Emotion: immersion. Pros: authority and depth. Risks: hard to keep alive; mobile typography care. Not for: transactional flows.

## 15 · Experimental (controlled)
Goal: be different without being confusing. Structure: one unusual idea anchored in familiar rest. Nav: reimagined but labeled. Density: varies. Rhythm: bold. Interaction: novel gesture/scroll/space with visible affordances. Emotion: surprise. Pros: memorability. Risks: comprehension, accessibility, budgets. Not for: high-stakes repeated tasks; always keep a conventional path.
